How to Use
01 · Choose Conversion Mode

Select True → Apparent to find the apparent dip in the tunnel from known plane orientation, or Apparent → True to recover the true dip from what is observed in the tunnel face.

02 · Enter Parameters

Use the sliders or type values directly into the number fields. True/Apparent Dip is 0–90°. Dip Direction & Tunnel Azimuth are 0–360° azimuths measured clockwise from North.

03 · Read the 3D Model

The interactive cube shows the rock mass (blue wireframe), tunnel cylinder at depth, and the geological plane (amber). Drag to rotate, scroll to zoom. Arrows on the surface mark dip direction and tunnel axis.

About this tool

When a planar geological feature — a bedding plane, joint set or fault — is exposed in a tunnel wall or face, the dip you measure is almost never the true dip. What you see is the apparent dip: the projection of the plane onto the tunnel's longitudinal section, which depends on the angle between the tunnel axis and the strike of the plane. This free calculator converts between the two in both directions, so you can predict how a known structure will appear in a planned drive, or recover the true orientation of a plane logged underground.

It is built for tunnelling and geotechnical engineers, engineering geologists and students working with structural geology, borehole and face mapping data. The relationship used is tan Ψ = tan α · sin δ, where Ψ is the apparent dip, α the true dip and δ the angle between the tunnel axis and the plane's strike (after Walsh, 2022, and Addie, 1968).
